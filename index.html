How empires shaped the words of Romania and Japan

A website using etymologies and loanwords in Japanese and Romanian to display the ultimately unifying aspects of languages that are often used as national identifiers in opposition to neighbors or other nearby language/cultural groups.

Japan

  1. Pre-history

    弟otouto

    little brother · Came from Old Japanese

    The word for little brother, “Otouto” derives from a combination of two older Japanese words, Oto and hito, meaning a young person.

    More words like this

    • 兄older brother
    • 妹little sister
    • 姉older sister
    • 父father
    • 母mother
    Read more in the blog

  2. 500s CE

    愛ai

    love · Came from Middle Chinese

    The word for love most commonly used today in Japan has its origins in medieval China where the Middle Chinese word for love first made its leap onto the Japanese archipelago through the extensive literature of the Chinese intelligentsia and religions, especially Buddhism.

Why this project

Language as a tool of nationalism is often if not universally used in contrast or opposition to other languages and “foreign influence” but this perception of a nation’s language as a native endeavor without outside input is completely impossible and illogical even in deeply isolationist/nationalized languages like Romanian and Japanese and people aren’t aware enough of that.
